# Home Fitness & Fat-Loss Plan — No Gym Required

**Profile:** 40 yrs • 175 cm • 85 kg → target ~77 kg
**Goals:** Lose belly fat / visible abs · Build calisthenics-level strength · Improve flexibility · Run longer without gassing out

---

## Split Days or Combined? (Your Question)

For where you're starting from, **combine everything into full-body sessions rather than a bodybuilder-style split** (leg day, chest day, etc.). Here's why:

- Split routines (leg day, chest day) are built for advanced lifters doing high training *volume* per muscle group — they need days off between to recover from that volume.
- You're not there yet, and your goals aren't "build bigger biceps" — they're fat loss, functional strength, endurance, and mobility. Full-body sessions 3x/week hit every muscle group more frequently, burn more calories per session, and fit a busy schedule far better.
- Cardio and strength on separate-ish days (with some overlap) works best for you — enough recovery between strength sessions, dedicated slots for running/HIIT so neither gets shortchanged.

So the week below is **full-body strength (3x) + dedicated cardio/HIIT (2-3x) + flexibility woven throughout**, not a body-part split.

One honest note on the "six-pack": you can't spot-reduce belly fat. Ab exercises strengthen the muscle underneath, but *visibility* comes almost entirely from overall body fat going down. The nutrition side of this plan is what gets you there — the core work just makes sure there's something defined underneath once the fat comes off.

## The Strength Sessions

### Full-Body Strength A — Push Focus (Mon)
1. Push-ups (knee push-ups if needed) — 3 × 8–12
2. Dumbbell shoulder press — 3 × 10–12
3. Dips on the power tower (use the assist/band, or bench dips if too hard at first) — 3 × 6–10
4. Goblet squats (dumbbell held at chest) — 3 × 12–15
5. Plank — 3 × 30–45 sec
6. Hand gripper — 2 × 15–20 squeezes per hand
Cool-down: 5 min stretch — chest, shoulders, quads

### Full-Body Strength B — Pull Focus (Wed)
1. Pull-ups / negative pull-ups / dead hangs on the tower — 3 × max effort (dead hang 20–30 sec if you can't do a rep yet)
2. Dumbbell bent-over rows — 3 × 10–12 per arm
3. Dumbbell Romanian deadlifts — 3 × 10–12
4. Reverse lunges — 3 × 10 per leg
5. Hanging knee raises on the tower — 3 × 10–15
6. Bicep curls — 2 × 12
Cool-down: stretch back, hamstrings, biceps

### Full-Body Strength C — Legs & Core (Fri)
1. Bodyweight or dumbbell-loaded squats — 3 × 15
2. Walking lunges with dumbbells — 3 × 10 per leg
3. Step-ups on a sturdy platform/stair — 3 × 10 per leg
4. Push-ups — 3 × 10–12
5. Hanging leg raises / captain's chair on the tower — 3 × 10–15
6. Side plank — 3 × 20–30 sec per side
Cool-down: stretch hip flexors, calves

## Cardio, HIIT & Endurance

**Tue — Zone-2 Cardio + Core:** Treadmill, 25–35 min brisk walk or light jog — pace where you could still hold a conversation. Finish with bicycle crunches 3×15, leg raises 3×12, plank 2×30 sec.

**Thu — Skip-Rope HIIT:** 30 sec fast skipping / 30 sec rest, 10–12 rounds (start with 20 sec on / 40 sec off if you're new to rope work). Then straight into 20 min of yoga/mobility on the mat.

**Sat — Long Endurance Day:** This is what builds "run longer without getting tired." Start at 30 min and add 5 min every 1–2 weeks toward 60 min. Mixing incline walking with jogging is easier on the knees than flat-out running and still torches calories — great option on the treadmill.

**Sun — Active Recovery:** 20–30 min full-body stretch, optional easy walk. No lifting, no HIIT.

## Nutrition

**Rough calorie target:** based on your stats, maintenance is approximately 2,350–2,450 kcal/day at light activity. A moderate deficit of ~400–500 kcal puts you around **1,900–2,000 kcal/day**, which should give roughly 0.4–0.5 kg/week of loss — enough to hit 77 kg in about 4–5 months without excessive muscle loss. Treat this as a starting estimate, not gospel — track your actual weight trend over 2 weeks and adjust up or down by ~100–150 kcal if it's moving too fast or not at all.

**Protein:** ~140–160 g/day, spread across meals (30–40 g each). This is the single biggest lever for keeping muscle while losing fat — eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, dal, tofu, Greek yogurt all work.

**Carbs & fats:** Favor complex carbs (oats, millets, brown rice, whole wheat, fruit, vegetables) around your workouts. Healthy fats (nuts, seeds, olive oil) round out the rest — no need to fear fat, just watch portions.

**Fiber:** ~30 g/day from vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains — keeps you full on fewer calories.

**Sample day shape:**
- Breakfast: eggs or paneer/oats + fruit
- Mid-morning: nuts or a piece of fruit
- Lunch: lean protein + vegetables + a whole grain (dal + roti/brown rice)
- Evening: sprouts, yogurt, or a protein shake
- Dinner: lighter protein + vegetables, go easy on carbs late
- Walk 10–15 min after lunch and dinner

**Favor:** lean protein, vegetables, fruit, whole grains, nuts/seeds in moderation, water.
**Limit:** sugary drinks, fried snacks, refined flour, alcohol, large meals right before bed.

## Daily Habits & Tips

- **Post-meal walks:** 10–15 min after your two main meals — helps blunt blood sugar spikes and aids digestion. Cheap, effective, easy to keep up.
- **Apple cider vinegar:** the evidence for it actually driving weight loss is weak/mixed — at best a small effect on appetite or blood sugar, nothing close to a shortcut. If you want to try it: 1–2 tsp diluted in a glass of water, not on an empty stomach if it irritates you, and rinse your mouth after (undiluted ACV can wear down tooth enamel over time). Don't count on it doing heavy lifting — training and food quality matter far more.
- **NEAT:** take stairs, stand during calls, park further away — this non-exercise movement adds up meaningfully over a week.
- **Warm up / cool down:** always, especially at 40 with some weight to lose — protects your knees and lower back.
- **Progress gradually:** you're coming from low activity, so resist the urge to do everything at max intensity in week 1. Consistency over 3 months beats a hard week followed by burnout or injury.

## Tracking Progress

- Weigh yourself 1–2x/week, same day/time — daily fluctuates too much to mean anything
- Waist measurement every 2 weeks — often the better fat-loss signal than the scale
- Progress photos monthly
- Log strength markers: max push-ups, pull-up progress, running time/distance — these keep you motivated even in weeks the scale doesn't move

## A Quick Safety Note

You mentioned normal blood pressure and general good health, which is a good starting point — but since you're coming from low activity, it's worth getting a general check-up before ramping into the running and heavier lifting portions, especially if it's been a while since your last one. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain (not the same as normal muscle fatigue), and progress load/intensity gradually rather than jumping in at full effort.
