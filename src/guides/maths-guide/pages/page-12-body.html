Ratio & proportion

Simplify ratios by dividing every part equally; proportional quantities use the same scale factor.

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two or more quantities. Written a : b or a : b : c. Order matters.

Boys : girls = 3 : 2 → for every 3 boys there are 2 girls

The whole is 3 + 2 = 5 parts, so boys are 35 of the class.

Simplify a ratio

Divide every part by the HCF.

12÷6 : 18÷6 → 2 : 3 10 : 15 : 20 → all ÷ 5 → 2 : 3 : 4

Units must match first (convert cm to m, etc.).

Sharing in a ratio — count the parts first

Two parts
Total parts → one part → each share

Share £56 in the ratio 3 : 4

Parts = 7 · one part = 56÷7 = 8 · shares = 24 : 32
Three parts
The same steps, with more parts

Share £60 in the ratio 3 : 2 : 1

Parts = 6 · one part = 10 → £30 : £20 : £10 Check: 30 + 20 + 10 = 60 ✓
Given one share
Find one part, then the other share

Boys : girls = 3 : 2. There are 12 boys.

3 parts = 12 → one part = 4 Girls = 2 × 4 = 8 · whole = 20

Unitary method

Find the value of 1, then multiply.

3 pens = £1.80 → 5 pens?

1 pen = 1.80 ÷ 3 = £0.60
5 pens = 0.60 × 5 = £3.00

Direct proportion

As one amount doubles, so does the other.

4 cakes need 200g flour → 10 cakes?

200 ÷ 4 = 50g each
50 × 10 = 500g

Scaling a recipe

Use one multiplier for every ingredient.

Serves 4 → serves 6 is × 1.5

300 ml → 450 ml
2 eggs → 3 eggs

Scale factors — two different jobs

Map scales
Real life shrunk onto paper

Map scale 1 : 25 000 means 1 cm on map = 25 000 cm = 250 m in real life.

4 cm on map → 4 × 250 m = 1 km Map → real: multiply · real → map: divide
Similar shapes
Same shape, all sides × the same factor

Angles stay equal. Find the factor from matching sides.

6 ÷ 3 = × 2 → x = 4 × 2 = 8 cm
Remember: A map scale shrinks real life onto paper; an enlargement keeps the shape. For “how many times bigger?”, divide: new ÷ old.
