Pull & grip exercises

Back, biceps and grip — and the ladder that takes you from zero to your first strict pull-up on the power tower.

The pull-up ladder — climb one rung at a time

1
Dead hang

Grip the bar, arms straight, feet off the floor, shoulders not scrunched into ears. Build to 3 × 20–30 s. Move on when: 30 s feels comfortable.

2
Scapular pull-up

From a dead hang, pull your shoulder blades down and back so your chest lifts a few centimetres — arms stay straight. 2–3 × 6–8. Move on when: 8 crisp reps.

3
Negative pull-up

Step or jump so your chin is over the bar, then lower yourself as slowly as you can (aim 3–5 s). 3 × 3–5. Move on when: five smooth 5-second lowers.

4
Band-assisted pull-up

Loop a resistance band over the bar and under a knee or foot; do full-range reps. 3 × 5–8, thinning the band over weeks. Move on when: 8 reps with the lightest band.

5
Full pull-up

From a dead hang, chin over the bar with no swing. Celebrate the first one — then collect singles through the day until 3–5 in a row arrive (milestones on p.12).

One-arm dumbbell row

lats · upper back · biceps · grip dumbbell + support
  1. Hinge forward with a flat back, one hand braced on the power tower or a sturdy chair.
  2. Pull the dumbbell to your hip pocket, elbow brushing your ribs.
  3. Lower with control to a full stretch at the bottom.
Start the pull with the shoulder blade, not the hand Hips stay square to the floor Twisting the torso or yanking the weight up
Easier: lighter · chest supported on an incline Harder: heavier · 1-s squeeze at the top

Dumbbell bicep curl

biceps · forearms dumbbells
  1. Stand tall, dumbbells at your sides, palms forward.
  2. Curl up without the elbows drifting forward or the hips swinging.
  3. Lower slowly to fully straight arms — no half reps.
Elbows pinned to the ribs Rocking the body to throw the weight up
Easier: alternate arms · lighter Harder: hammer grip (palms in) · 3-s lowers

Hand gripper

grip · forearms hand gripper
  1. Squeeze to a full close, hold one second.
  2. Release under control — don't let it spring open.
  3. 2 × 15–20 per hand at the end of Workout A, or while watching TV.
Full range every rep Daily max-effort sessions — grip needs recovery too, and fried forearms sabotage pull-up day
Easier: lower resistance setting Harder: slow negatives · timed holds at full close
Why grip matters: dead hangs, rows and the gripper quietly build the forearm strength that decides whether pull-ups, heavy carries and “lifting heavy stuff” feel possible. It's the cheapest strength you'll ever buy.
