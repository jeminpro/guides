Core exercises

Anti-sag, anti-twist, controlled movement — this is what builds a core that's strong and defined once the body fat comes down.

Plank

deep abs · shoulders · glutes mat
  1. Forearms under shoulders, legs extended, toes tucked.
  2. Squeeze glutes, tuck the pelvis slightly and brace as if about to be poked in the stomach.
  3. Hold a dead-straight line from head to heels — and keep breathing.
Push the floor away through the forearms Hips sagging or piking; holding your breath. End the set when form breaks, not the timer
Easier: knees down · hands on a raised surface Harder: 60-s holds · slow shoulder taps · feet elevated

Side plank

obliques · hips mat
  1. Elbow directly under the shoulder, feet stacked or staggered.
  2. Lift the hips until you're one straight line, top arm on your hip or reaching up.
  3. Hold, then swap sides — always both sides.
Hips high and stacked Sagging toward the floor or rolling the chest forward
Easier: bottom knee bent on the floor Harder: 45-s+ holds · raise the top leg

Dead bug

deep abs · lower-back control mat
  1. On your back, arms to the ceiling, knees stacked over hips at 90°.
  2. Press the lower back gently into the mat and keep it there.
  3. Slowly lower opposite arm and leg toward the floor, exhale, return, swap sides.
Slower is better — 3 seconds out, 3 back The lower back popping off the mat — shorten the range if it does
Easier: move legs only (heel taps) Harder: straighter legs · a light dumbbell in the moving hand

Hanging knee raise → leg raise

lower abs · hip flexors · grip power tower
  1. Dead hang (or use the tower's forearm pads — captain's chair — while grip catches up).
  2. Tilt the pelvis and curl the knees up to hip height or higher, without swinging.
  3. Lower slowly to fully straight — no dropping.
Curl the hips up, don't just lift the thighs Kipping and swinging between reps — reset the hang if you start pendulum-ing
Easier: captain's chair · lying leg raises below Harder: straight-leg raise to horizontal · toes-to-bar over months

Lying leg raise

lower abs · hip flexors mat
  1. Flat on your back, legs straight, hands under your hips if the lower back complains.
  2. Raise legs to vertical, keeping them together.
  3. Lower slowly and stop before the lower back starts to arch off the mat.
Lower back pressed down the entire rep Letting the heels crash to the floor
Easier: bent knees · smaller range Harder: slower lowers · ankle weights eventually

Bicycle crunch

abs · obliques mat
  1. On your back, hands lightly behind your head, knees up.
  2. Extend one leg while rotating the opposite elbow toward the bent knee.
  3. Alternate sides in a slow, deliberate pedal — quality over speed.
Rotate from the ribs; elbows stay wide Yanking the neck with your hands or racing through reps
Easier: feet tap the floor between reps Harder: slower tempo · legs hover lower (back stays flat)
Truth about six-packs: you cannot spot-reduce belly fat — 500 crunches a day won't do it. These exercises build the muscle underneath; visibility comes from the calorie deficit (p.13). Do both and the flat stomach follows.
