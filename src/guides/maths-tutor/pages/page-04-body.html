× works both ways

Swap the numbers round — same answer! So you only learn half the chart.

7 × 8 = 56  and  8 × 7 = 56

÷ is the opposite of ×

Every times-table fact gives you two dividing facts free.

56 ÷ 8 = 7  and  56 ÷ 7 = 8

Multiplying & dividing in your head

1 · Doubling & halving
Double it, then double again

×4 = double twice · ×8 = double 3 times · ÷4 = halve twice.

35 × 4 → 70 → 140  ·  96 ÷ 4 → 48 → 24
2 · Partitioning
Split it up, then add

Split into tens and ones, times each part, then add them.

23 × 6 → (20×6) + (3×6) → 120 + 18 = 138
3 · ×10 / ×100 & multiples of 10
Every digit moves along

×10 → 1 place left · ÷10 → 1 place right — not “add a 0”.

4.7 × 100 = 470  ·  30 × 40 = 1200
4 · Related facts
Use a fact you already know

×5 = ×10 then halve · ×9 = ×10 then take one lot off.

28 × 5 → 280 ÷ 2 = 140  ·  7 × 9 → 70 − 7 = 63

Patterns that help you remember ×6, ×7 and ×8 are the ones people forget

×2 Double it — always even7 × 2 = 14
×5 Answer ends in 5 or 06 × 5 = 30
×10 Every digit moves 1 place left8 × 10 = 80 · 4.7 × 10 = 47
×6 Do ×3, then double it6 × 7 → 21 → 42
×7 Do ×5, then add ×28 × 7 → 40 + 16 = 56
×8 Double three times7 × 8 → 14 → 28 → 56
×9 Digits add up to 9 (up to ×10)4 × 9 = 36 → 3 + 6 = 9
×11 Write the digit twice (up to ×9)7 × 11 = 77
×12 Do ×10 and ×2, then add7 × 12 = 70 + 14 = 84
