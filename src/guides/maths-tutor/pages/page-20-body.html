Multi-step? Underline each fact, solve one step at a time, then check with an estimate. A second fraction or percentage is nearly always taken from the new amount.

Multi-step challenges

Fraction of the rest

Ella has £45. She spends 25 on a book, then 13 of the rest on lunch. What is left?

  1. Book: 45 ÷ 5 × 2 = £18
  2. Left: 45 − 18 = £27
  3. Lunch: 27 ÷ 3 = £9
27 − 9 = £18
Two percentages

An £80 coat is reduced by 25%, then a further 10% off the sale price. What do you pay?

  1. 25% of 80 = £20
  2. Sale price: 80 − 20 = £60
  3. 10% of 60 = £6 (not of 80!)
60 − 6 = £54
Ratio from a difference

Sweets are shared 3 : 5 between Amy and Ben. Ben gets 12 more than Amy. How many sweets in total?

  1. Difference = 5 − 3 = 2 parts
  2. 2 parts = 12 → 1 part = 6
  3. Total = 3 + 5 = 8 parts
8 × 6 = 48 sweets
Back from a mean

The mean of 5 numbers is 12. A sixth number makes the mean 13. What is the sixth number?

  1. Old total = 5 × 12 = 60
  2. New total = 6 × 13 = 78
  3. Extra number = the gap
78 − 60 = 18
Perimeter → area

A rectangle has perimeter 36 cm and its length is twice its width. What is its area?

  1. Half perimeter = 18 cm
  2. w + 2w = 18 → w = 6 cm
  3. Length = 2 × 6 = 12 cm
6 × 12 = 72 cm²
Best buy

Pens are £1.20 each or 5 for £5. Jack needs 12 and buys them as cheaply as he can. What does he save?

  1. All singles: 12 × 1.20 = £14.40
  2. 2 packs = 10 pens for £10
  3. 2 singles more = £2.40
14.40 − 12.40 = £2

Venn diagrams

30 children: 18 play football (A), 14 swim (B), 8 do both. Fill the overlap first, then the “only” regions, then outside.

Both = 8 Only A = 18 − 8 = 10 Only B = 14 − 8 = 6 Neither = 30 − (10+8+6) = 6
