Place value & number sense

A digit’s value is the digit × its place value. One place left is ×10; one place right is ÷10.

Compare numbers

1

Count the digits. For whole numbers, more digits means bigger. 5,847 vs 962 → 5,847 wins

2

Same length? Compare left to right - start with the leftmost digit. The first place they differ decides which is bigger.

3

Decimals? If the whole parts match, compare after the point left to right - tenths, hundredths… The greater digit wins. 0.58 vs 0.6 → 0.6 wins

Rounding

1
Find the place

Spot the place value you’re rounding to - ones, tens, hundreds… Circle that digit.

2
Look next door

Check the digit immediately to its right - this neighbour decides everything.

3
Decide

0–4 keep the circled digit · 5–9 add 1 to it.

4
Change everything after

Whole numbers: turn digits after into 0
Decimals: remove those digits

Addition (+)

  • Line ones under ones. Start on the right.
  • Ones: 7 + 6 = 13 → write 3, carry 1.
  • Tens: 1 + 8 + 5 = 14 → write 4, carry 1.
  • Hundreds: 1 + 4 + 3 = 8.

Subtraction (−)

  • Line ones under ones. Start on the right.
  • Ones: borrow 1 ten from the 4. The 4 becomes 3, and the 3 becomes 13. Then 13 − 6 = 7.
  • Tens: 3 is too small, so borrow 1 hundred from the 8. The 8 becomes 7, and the 3 becomes 13. Then 13 − 5 = 8.
  • Hundreds: 7 − 3 = 4.

Multiplication (×)

  • Ignore the decimal for now: work with 258 × 75.
  • Multiply by the ones digit (5) → get 1290.
  • Multiply by the tens digit (7), put a 0 on the end first → get 18060.
  • Add those two answers together.
  • Count decimal places in the starting numbers (here: 1) and put the point that many digits from the right.

Division (÷)

117 ÷ 5 = 1175
  • How many times does 5 fit into the first digits? (11 ÷ 5 = 2)
  • Write that on top. Multiply (2 × 5 = 10), then subtract.
  • Bring down the next digit and do the same again.
  • Still some left over? Add a decimal point and a 0, then keep going.
  • When nothing is left over, you’re done - line the decimal points up!
