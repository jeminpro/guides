Angle rules

Angles total 180° on a straight line and in a triangle, 360° around a point and in a quadrilateral.

Types of angle

0° < a < 90°
Acute

Sharp / smaller than a right angle

a = 90°
Right

Shown with a square corner mark

90° < a < 180°
Obtuse

Wider than a right angle, less than straight

180° < a < 360°
Reflex

Bigger than a straight line

Straight line = 180° · Full turn = 360°. Reflex of an angle = 360° − that angle.

Straight line

Angles on a straight line = 180°

a + b = 180°

Around a point

Angles around a point = 360°

a + b + c + d = 360°

Triangle

Interior angles = 180°

Equilateral → each 60° Right-angled → other two sum to 90°

Vertically opposite

Opposite angles at a cross are equal

If one angle is 70°, the opposite is 70°

Parallel lines (transversal)

Corresponding (F)

Same position at each crossing.

a = b

Alternate (Z)

Inside, opposite sides of the transversal.

a = b

Co-interior (C)

Inside, same side of the transversal.

a + b = 180°

Arrows mark the parallel lines. Only use these three rules once you have spotted the arrows.

Polygon interior angles

ShapeSides (n)Sum of interiorsRegular: each interiorRegular: each exterior
Triangle3180°60°120°
Quadrilateral4360°90°90°
Pentagon5540°108°72°
Hexagon6720°120°60°

Sum of interiors = (n − 2) × 180°. Exteriors of any polygon sum to 360°, so a regular one has each exterior = 360° ÷ n. At every vertex interior + exterior = 180°, so each interior = 180° − (360° ÷ n).

Exam tip: Label everything you know first. Look for straight lines, triangles, and parallel marks (arrows).
